What it is

One cohort. Two departments. Four courses that actually talk to each other.

Most students take business here and technology there, and never connect them. AI Innovators pairs them on purpose. Your marketing assignment and your AI assignment are the same project, graded from two angles.

  • You move through all four courses with the same classmates — the same faces, all year.
  • Every month has an AI touchpoint: market research, competitor analysis, financial projections, pitch deck.
  • Faculty from Business and Computer Information Systems teach in coordination, not in parallel.
  • Industry guests come to you — working professionals across the year.

The sequence

Fall builds the venture. Spring takes it to market.

Courses run in short late-start blocks so they fit around work and other classes.

Fall 2026 · late Oct – Dec

MGMT 80

Small Business Entrepreneurship

Find a problem worth solving. Size the market. Build the business model.

Fall 2026 · late Oct – Dec

AI 45

Generative AI Applications

Spring 2027 · Feb – Apr

MKTG 47

Essentials of Marketing

Position it, price it, and plan how real customers will hear about it.

Spring 2027 · Feb – Apr

AI 40

Go under the hood: how these systems work, where they fail, and the ethics of using them in business.

What you leave with

Four things you can show someone.

01 / Units

12 transferable units

Four CSU-transferable courses that count toward Business and CIS certificates and degrees. Nothing here is a detour from your plan.

02 / Credential

An industry certification

Exam vouchers are available to eligible enrolled students while grant funding lasts, so you can sit for a recognized AI or business credential at no cost to you.

03 / Portfolio

A finished pitch

A complete business plan and pitch deck you built yourself, with AI in the workflow — the kind of artifact that answers "what have you actually done?"

04 / People

Industry guest speakers, a panel of external judges, and 25–35 classmates who know your work. Networks start somewhere.

The capstone

May 2027: you get the room, the judges, and five minutes.

Student teams present AI-driven business plans to a panel of outside judges — entrepreneurs, technologists, and small-business leaders from the Long Beach region. They score against a rubric, ask hard questions, and stay afterward to talk.

How the pitch works

Before the main event: a December 2026 internal showcase gives you a lower-stakes run at it — same format, friendly room, faculty and peers only.

Judges score on problem clarity, market evidence, financial reasoning, responsible AI use, and delivery.

Who it's for

If you've ever had an idea and no idea what to do next, this is built for you.

You do not need a business background. You do not need to know anything about AI. You do not need a finished idea when you start.

  • Open to all currently enrolled LBCC students in good standing.
  • No prerequisites beyond standard course requirements.
  • Designed with working students in mind — late-start blocks and one cohort schedule.
  • Counselors and CTE advisors can refer you; you can also sign yourself up.

Standard LBCC enrollment fees apply. The program itself adds no additional cost, and fee waivers you already qualify for still apply.
