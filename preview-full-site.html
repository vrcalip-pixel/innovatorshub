What it is

One cohort. Two departments. Four courses that actually talk to each other.

Most students take business here and technology there, and never connect them. AI Innovators pairs them on purpose. Your marketing assignment and your AI assignment are the same project, graded from two angles.

  • You move through all four courses with the same classmates — the same faces, all year.
  • Every month has an AI touchpoint: market research, competitor analysis, financial projections, pitch deck.
  • Faculty from Business and Computer Information Systems teach in coordination, not in parallel.
  • Industry guests come to you — working professionals across the year.

Monthly AI touchpoints

Where the two sides meet.

These are not separate AI exercises. Each one is a business deliverable you owe your course anyway — done with AI in the workflow, then checked by you.

Month 1

Market research

Use AI to scan an industry and size an opportunity, then verify what it told you against real sources. You'll find at least one thing it got wrong. That's the assignment.

Month 2

Competitor analysis

Map who else is solving this problem and where the gap is. Build the visual analysis your pitch will lean on.

Month 3

Financial projections

Revenue model, cost structure, and a cash flow you can explain line by line. AI can build the model; you have to own the assumptions.

Month 4

Pitch deck

Turn a plan into a five-minute story. Draft, cut, rehearse, cut again.

The same four-part arc runs again in Spring with a marketing lens, at a higher level of difficulty, on the same venture.

Tools

What you'll be working in.

  • ChatGPT Enterprise — provided through LBCC's institutional license. No personal subscription required.
  • Canva Pro — already available to LBCC students, used for visual analysis and pitch decks.
  • Survey and analytics tools — for customer research and validating your assumptions.

On academic integrity: AI use is expected here, not hidden. You'll be asked to document how you used it, what you changed, and what you rejected. Learning to disclose your process is part of the professional skill.

Questions

Before you sign up.

Do I need experience with AI or business?

No. Both course pairs start from the beginning. What you need is willingness to work on one idea for a year.

Does this cost extra?

No. You pay standard LBCC enrollment fees for the four courses, and any fee waiver you already qualify for still applies. There is no additional program fee, and course materials are provided.

Do these courses count toward my degree?

Yes. All four are CSU-transferable and apply to existing LBCC Business and Computer Information Systems certificates and degrees. Check with a counselor for how they map to your specific plan.

Can I work while I'm in this?

Yes — it's designed for that. Courses run in short late-start blocks rather than spanning the full term, and assignments are coordinated across courses so you aren't doing the same research twice. Tell us on the interest form if you need advance notice for scheduling.

What's the deadline?

The interest list closes Friday, October 16, 2026 at 10:00 AM. You'll hear back by October 19, and the kickoff is October 23.

Do I need my own business idea to start?

No. Some students arrive with one. Others find theirs in the first few weeks. Teams form during the fall.

Certifications

A credential on your record, not just a grade.

Course credit shows you took a class. An industry certification shows an outside body tested you and you passed. AI Innovators covers the exam fee so cost isn't the reason you skip it.

How it works

Three steps.

Step 1

Enroll and stay active

Vouchers are available to eligible CTE students enrolled in the learning community. You don't apply separately.

Step 2

Prepare in class

Exam preparation is built into coursework and supported with practice materials. You won't be studying alone on your own time.

Step 3

Sit for the exam

Faculty coordinate scheduling and the voucher covers the exam fee. Pass, and the credential is yours to keep.

The honest version: vouchers are funded by a grant, in a fixed quantity, for the 2026–27 year. They're available to eligible enrolled students while funding lasts. If demand exceeds supply, faculty will publish how spots are allocated before anyone has to compete for one.

How you're scored

The rubric, in plain terms.

Problem clarity

Is there a real problem, and can you show someone actually has it?

Market evidence

Do your numbers come from somewhere, and can you name the source?

Financial reasoning

Does the money work, and do you understand your own assumptions?

Responsible AI use

Can you show where AI helped, where you overrode it, and why?

Delivery

Five minutes, clear story, confident under questions.

Team

Does the work hold together, and does everyone know their part of it?

The full rubric is shared with students at the start of Fall, not held back until the end. You should know exactly what you're being measured on.

Industry professionals from entrepreneurship, technology, and small business, recruited through LBCC's industry advisory committees, regional chambers of commerce, and faculty networks.

Judges score against the rubric, give each team direct verbal feedback, and stay afterward for open networking with students.

About

Two departments decided to stop teaching this separately.

Employers on LBCC's advisory committees kept saying the same thing: they need graduates who understand AI in a business context, not one or the other. AI Innovators is the Business and Computer Information Systems departments' answer to that.

The model

A learning community is more than a shared schedule.

Co-enrolling students in two courses is the easy part. The work is in the coordination: shared assignments, a joint rubric, integration points mapped month by month, and faculty who plan together instead of teaching side by side.

Students move through the full year as one group. That continuity is the intervention. It's what turns a class into a cohort and a cohort into a network.

What we're testing

This is a pilot, and we mean it.

  • Does integrated AI-and-business instruction produce stronger outcomes than teaching them apart?
  • Does the cohort model improve persistence from Fall into Spring?
  • Can a coordinated, cross-departmental design be documented well enough for other programs to adopt?

Findings and the curriculum framework will be published at the close of the grant year so other colleges can use them.

Funding

How this is paid for.

AI Innovators is supported by a 2026–27 Workforce Education grant from the California Community Colleges Strong Workforce Program, administered through LBCC's Workforce Education office.

Grant funds cover curriculum development, faculty professional development, instructional materials and equipment, certification exam vouchers, and capstone event operations. They do not fund payments to students.
